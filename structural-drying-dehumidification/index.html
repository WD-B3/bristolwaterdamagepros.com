Structural drying and dehumidification is the process of removing moisture from a building's framing, floors and walls after water damage. It is the stage that follows water removal, and it is also the stage that prevents mold and warping when it is done right.

This page is about the drying process itself. Standing water is handled first under water extraction, and damp materials that cannot be dried are covered under wall and drywall repair.

Walls that still feel damp days later

Many homeowners search for help when a wall still feels cool or damp days after a leak. Others notice a musty smell after the carpet seems dry, or windows that fog in a room that had a leak. These are the signs that surface drying is not the same as the structure drying. Moisture hides in framing, insulation and subfloor, where fans and open windows cannot reach it.

The fix is a measured process, not more fans. A moisture meter and thermal camera show where water remains, and equipment is placed to pull it out.

Moisture mapping and the equipment

A crew starts with moisture mapping: readings taken across walls, floors and ceilings to find the wet boundary. They then place air movers to push moving air across wet surfaces and LGR dehumidifiers to pull the moisture from the air. In sealed or tight spaces, specialized drying panels or desiccant units may be used.

  • Moisture meters and thermal imaging to find wet areas.
  • Air movers that move air across wet materials.
  • LGR dehumidifiers that collect water from the air.
  • Daily readings to adjust equipment as areas dry.

Equipment is placed by the readings, which is why renting a few fans from a hardware store rarely finishes the job. The crew rechecks readings each day and removes equipment only when the structure is back to normal.

Water mitigation: the term behind the work

Restoration pros use the word water mitigation for the steps that limit damage: stop the source, remove water, dry the structure and remove what cannot be saved. Mitigation is the emergency stage, and repair and rebuilding come afterward. The homepage covers the full first 72 hours, and this page explains the drying portion.

Drying time depends on the materials, the temperature and the water category. Hardwood and plaster dry slowly, concrete holds moisture, and carpet and drywall dry faster if caught early. The crew sets the schedule by the readings, and commonly the equipment runs for several days.

Equipment you will see

Air movers are the small fans that push air across surfaces, and low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers pull moisture from the air. Specialty systems force dry air into wall cavities or under floors through small holes. Each device has a job, and a crew chooses and places them based on readings.

Moisture meters, thermal cameras and hygrometers are the measurement tools. They guide where equipment goes and when it can come out.

What to do while the equipment runs

  • Keep doors and windows closed unless the crew says otherwise.
  • Do not unplug or move the equipment.
  • Keep the heat or air conditioning at a steady setting.
  • Report any new smell or damp spot to the crew.

Drying goals and monitoring follow ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration.

Common questions about structural drying in Bristol

How do I know when drying is finished?

When moisture readings in the walls and floors match the readings in unaffected areas of your home. A crew should show you the numbers.

Can I dry a wet wall with fans alone?

Fans move surface air but do not pull moisture from inside a wall. Without dehumidification and readings, hidden moisture often remains.

Does drying prevent mold?

Fast, complete drying removes the moisture mold needs. No company can promise that mold will stay away.
