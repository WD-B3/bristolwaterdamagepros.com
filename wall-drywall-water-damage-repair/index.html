Wall and Drywall Water Damage in Bristol, RI

A wall can look fine and still be wet inside. We handle wall and drywall water damage repair in Bristol, RI.

Wall and drywall water damage repair is the drying and replacement of drywall, plaster, baseboards and insulation after water reaches a wall. Drywall is paper and gypsum, so it absorbs water, softens and can stain or grow mold if it stays wet.

This page is about the wall as the affected material. Water that came through a ceiling is covered under ceiling water damage repair, and the drying equipment is described at structural drying.

Signs a wall is wet inside

Look for bubbling paint, soft or crumbly spots at the baseboard, a stain that grows, peeling wallpaper or a musty smell. A wall can be wet behind the surface when only a small mark shows. A moisture meter confirms it without cutting.

Do not paint over a stain. Paint traps moisture and the stain returns. Photograph the wall and mark the edge of the stain with a pencil so you can see if it grows.

Drywall or plaster: why it matters in Bristol

Many older Bristol homes have plaster walls over wood lath, which is thicker and dries slowly. Plaster can sometimes be dried and patched, while drywall that is soaked or soft is usually cut out. Insulation in the wall cavity is replaced when it is wet.

Baseboards, trim and the bottom foot or two of a wall are the first to wick water. When water runs in from a leak above, the damage can be higher on the wall. A crew uses a moisture meter and thermal camera to find the wet boundary and cuts the wall where readings show trapped water.

How the repair is done

The crew cuts out damaged sections, removes wet insulation, dries the framing with air movers and dehumidifiers and confirms readings. New drywall or plaster is installed, then taped, textured, primed and painted. Matching paint on an older wall may mean painting the whole wall.

  • Moisture mapping with a meter and thermal camera.
  • Cutting out wet drywall and insulation.
  • Drying wall cavities and framing.
  • Patching, texture and paint after drying.

Painting and finishing after repair

After new drywall is hung, joints are taped and mudded, sanded and primed. A stain-blocking primer helps if tannins or rust remain on nearby surfaces. Painting an entire wall rather than a patch gives the best color match. On an older plaster wall, a skim coat can blend the repair into the original surface.

Allow each coat to dry fully. Rushing the finish can trap moisture and cause bubbling or cracking.

Insulation and vapor considerations

When a wall cavity is opened, it is a good time to check the insulation and any vapor barrier. Wet insulation is replaced, and a contractor can advise whether the existing type is right for the wall and local climate.

Do not remove large sections of wall yourself before a crew has taken readings. Cutting too much adds cost, while cutting too little leaves moisture behind.

Keep the room's air moving with the crew's equipment, and avoid hanging pictures on a wall that is still being dried.

Wall drying goals follow ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration.

Common questions about wall and drywall water damage in Bristol

Can wet drywall be dried instead of replaced?

Sometimes, if it is clean water and caught fast. Soft, crumbly or stained drywall is usually replaced.

How do I know if a wall is still wet?

A moisture meter reads the wall without cutting it.

Is it ok to cover a stain with primer?

Not until the wall is dry and the leak is fixed.
