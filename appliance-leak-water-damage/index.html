Appliance leak water damage is the wetting of floors and walls after a washing machine, dishwasher, refrigerator or similar appliance fails. The water is usually Category 1 or 2, and the damage is often hidden behind or under the machine until a stain or smell shows up.

This page owns the appliance failure. Damage to cabinets and the sink area is covered under kitchen water damage, and a leaking heater has its own page.

Appliance leak emergency: stop the water first

Shut the supply valves to the appliance, which are usually behind a washer or under a sink, and unplug the machine only if the floor around the outlet is dry. If you cannot reach the valves, close the main water valve. Do not run the appliance again until a technician checks it.

Move rugs and furniture off wet floors and open cabinet doors to let air in. If water has run toward an outlet, a baseboard heater or the panel, stay out and call the utility or 911 first, then call dispatch.

The usual failures and where the water goes

Washing machine hoses fail at the fittings or burst under pressure, and a drain hose popped off the standpipe can dump a full wash load on the floor. Dishwashers leak from door gaskets, supply lines and drain hoses, and the water often goes under the cabinet toe-kick and into the subfloor.

Refrigerator ice-maker lines and water dispensers leak slowly. A thin line behind a fridge can drip for weeks, and the first sign is usually warped flooring or a musty smell near the kitchen. Pull the appliance out and look for a damp floor before it becomes a larger repair.

What the cleanup involves

Crews extract surface water, check the subfloor and adjoining wall with a moisture meter and remove wet baseboard, laminate or pad that cannot be dried. Appliance leaks are often slow, so the first step is mapping how far the moisture spread beyond the visible stain.

  • Moisture mapping around and under the appliance.
  • Extraction and removal of unsalvageable flooring layers.
  • Drying under cabinets and behind toe-kicks.
  • Check of the wall and floor above or below.

Hoses and fittings worth replacing

Rubber washer hoses age and can burst without warning. Braided stainless hoses last longer, and replacing them every few years is inexpensive compared with the damage a burst can cause. Check the dishwasher supply line, the ice maker line and the water heater connectors at the same time.

If you are leaving for more than a few days, close the washer valves. A closed valve costs nothing and prevents a flood in an empty house.

What to do before help arrives

  • Shut off the valve to the appliance and unplug it if the floor is dry.
  • Do not use a wet appliance or touch wet outlets.
  • Move items off the wet floor and open windows.
  • Photograph the appliance label and the damage.

Water category and drying goals for an appliance loss are set under ANSI/IICRC S500, the standard for professional water damage restoration.

Appliance repair

Fixing or replacing the appliance is not part of restoration.

A sudden appliance failure is often treated as covered, while a leak that ran for a long time is often excluded for neglect. Check your policy, ask your insurer and photograph the appliance and the damage. No claim outcome is promised.

Common questions about appliance leak water damage in Bristol

Is a small appliance leak worth calling about?

Yes if the floor stayed wet for more than a day. A moisture reading tells you whether water is under the flooring or in the wall.

What if the leak went to the floor below?

Call at once. A ceiling holding water can drop, so see ceiling guidance and keep clear until a crew checks it.

Should I replace the hose myself?

Replacing a hose is a plumbing task, and drying should start first so the floor does not stay wet.

Do you handle appliance leak emergencies at night or on weekends in Bristol?

Yes. Dispatch is answered 24/7, including nights and weekends, and same-day response is available when a crew is free. Close the appliance supply valves while you wait.
