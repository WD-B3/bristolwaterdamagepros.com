Commercial water damage restoration is the removal of water and drying of offices, shops, restaurants and multi-unit buildings after a leak, flood or storm. The work is the same science as in a home, but the schedule, documentation and tenant coordination are different.

This page is for businesses and multi-unit properties. A single-family home with the same problem is better served by water extraction or flood damage cleanup, depending on the cause.

Commercial water emergency: protect the business first

Shut off the main water valve for the building or the affected unit, keep people and customers out of wet areas and turn off power to wet circuits only if the panel is dry and reachable. If water touches electrical equipment or the building has a gas leak, call the utility or 911 before calling us.

Move inventory, files and electronics off the floor, and photograph the damage and any equipment affected. Call dispatch so a coordinator can ask about the building, the tenants and the downtime.

How water moves through a mixed-use building

Many Bristol businesses sit in older mixed-use buildings along Hope Street and Metacom Avenue, with apartments or offices above shops. Water from a unit above runs down through floors and ceilings into other tenants' spaces, so a single failure can involve several parties.

Older buildings often have plaster ceilings, wood floors and hidden chases that carry water between floors. Large flat roofs and commercial HVAC can add leak points. A crew maps moisture on every floor involved, not only where the first stain appeared.

What a commercial plan covers

The plan starts with the same steps as any water loss: stop the source, extract, map moisture and dry. It adds scheduling around business hours, phased work to keep part of the space open when possible and clear records for the owner, the tenant and their insurers.

  • Extraction and drying sequenced to reopen the space sooner.
  • Moisture logs and photos for property and business insurers.
  • Coordination between building owner, tenants and contractors.
  • Removal of wet ceiling tiles, insulation and wall materials.

Contaminated water falls under sewage backup cleanup, and the equipment stage is described in structural drying.

Planning for downtime

A business loses money while it is closed, so the plan starts with what can stay open. Crews can dry one area at a time, work after hours and set up containment so customers and staff can use the rest of the building. Tell dispatch your hours and what must keep running.

Keep a record of lost revenue and extra expenses. If you carry business interruption coverage, your insurer will ask for those figures.

Ask your property manager or landlord who is responsible for common areas. Responsibility for repairs and for tenant contents is often split, and the lease or policy decides who pays.

What to do while you wait for a commercial crew

  • Shut the water supply and protect electronics and records.
  • Keep staff and customers out of wet areas.
  • Move inventory off the floor if it is safe.
  • Photograph damage and keep a list of affected items.

Commercial jobs follow ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration, for water categories and drying goals.

Common questions about commercial water damage in Bristol

Can you work around business hours?

Yes. The crew plans phasing and times with the owner, and some work can be done after hours.

Who is responsible when water comes from another unit?

That depends on leases and policies. The restoration crew documents each unit's damage so owners and insurers can sort it out.

What records should a business keep?

Dated photos, an inventory of damaged stock and equipment, moisture logs and receipts for emergency steps. Ask your insurer what it requires.

Do you handle commercial water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Bristol?

Yes. Dispatch is answered 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays, and same-day response is available when a crew is free. Tell the coordinator the building type and how many tenants are affected.
