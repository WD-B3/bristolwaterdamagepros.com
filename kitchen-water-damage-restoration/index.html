Kitchen Water Damage in Bristol, RI

Kitchens hold a lot of plumbing in a small space, so a leak spreads into cabinets and floors quickly. We handle kitchen water damage restoration in Bristol, RI.

Kitchen water damage restoration is the drying and repair of cabinets, countertops, flooring and walls after water escapes from a sink, supply line, dishwasher connection or ice-maker line. Cabinets made of particleboard swell and fall apart when they stay wet, so speed matters more here than in most rooms.

This page is about the room and its cabinetry. The appliance itself is the focus of appliance leak damage, and a split pipe in the wall is covered at burst pipe damage.

Water under the sink: signs and first steps

A leak under a kitchen sink often shows as a swollen cabinet base, a musty smell, peeling laminate or a soft spot in the floor in front of the sink. Shut the angle stops under the sink, and shut the main valve if they will not turn. Empty the cabinet and photograph the damage before moving anything.

If water has run toward an outlet or appliance, stay clear and turn off the circuit only if the panel is dry. A slow drip can be present for weeks, and that long-term moisture is the reason a thorough moisture check matters.

How kitchen leaks spread

Water follows the toe-kick under cabinets into the subfloor and travels along the floor to the next room. The back of a cabinet can stay wet behind a dishwasher or fridge. A leaking supply line inside a wall may soak insulation that you cannot see.

Many Bristol kitchens were remodeled over older framing, and a newer cabinet face can hide a damp wall behind it. Older homes may have plaster behind the cabinets, which dries slowly. That is why readings, not appearance, decide what stays.

What can be saved and what cannot

Solid wood cabinets are often dried in place, while particleboard boxes that swell are usually replaced. Granite and quartz countertops are not damaged by water, but the cabinet base under them can be. Laminate and engineered flooring that swells is typically removed, and hardwood is assessed for cupping.

  • Moisture mapping of cabinets, floor and the adjoining wall.
  • Removal of swollen particleboard and wet toe-kicks.
  • Drying under and behind cabinets with air movers and dehumidifiers.
  • Repair or replacement of cabinets and flooring after drying.

Floors damaged by the leak follow hardwood floor water damage, and the wall behind the cabinets is covered in wall and drywall repair.

Appliances, plumbing and the cabinet toe-kick

A refrigerator ice maker line and a dishwasher supply line are the two most common kitchen leak sources after the sink. Pull the appliance forward and check the floor behind it. The toe-kick under the cabinets is a hidden channel where water travels, and removing it lets the crew dry underneath.

Replacing a flexible supply line that is cracked or bulging is inexpensive. Doing it before a failure avoids a costly cleanup.

Countertops and backsplashes

Laminate countertops swell where water gets under the edge, while stone and solid surface tops tolerate water well. If a countertop must be detached to dry the cabinet, a contractor can often reinstall it. Sealed seams and backsplash caulk keep water from running behind.

What to do before help arrives

  • Shut the angle stops or the main valve.
  • Do not use wet outlets or appliances.
  • Empty the cabinet and lift items off the wet floor.
  • Photograph the cabinet, floor and plumbing.

Water categories and drying goals for a kitchen loss follow ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration.

What shapes the cost of this work

Kitchen costs follow the cabinets, because cabinetry is the biggest rebuild item.

Cabinet material

Particleboard is usually replaced, while solid wood may be saved.

Leak duration

A slow leak damages more of the base and the floor.

Flooring

Laminate and hardwood respond differently than tile.

Hidden water

Water behind appliances and in walls adds opening and drying.

A sudden plumbing leak is often covered, while a slow leak that continued for a long time is often not. Check your policy and ask your insurer, and photograph the damage first. No claim outcome is promised.

Common questions about kitchen water damage in Bristol

Can swollen cabinets be dried and saved?

Solid wood sometimes, particleboard rarely. A moisture reading and an inspection decide.

Do I need to remove the countertop?

Only if the cabinet base under it must be replaced.

Is the water under the sink a plumbing problem or a restoration problem?

Both. A plumber fixes the leak, and a restoration crew dries what got wet.
