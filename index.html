What water damage restoration in Bristol involves

Water damage restoration is the work of removing water, drying the structure and repairing what the water ruined. It begins with stopping the source and ends with a home that has dried to normal moisture readings, not just a floor that looks dry. Bristol sits on Narragansett Bay, so the cause can be a failed supply line on Hope Street, a sump pump that quit during a nor'easter or a harbor-side storm surge.

The first 24 to 72 hours decide how large the job becomes. Wet drywall, insulation and carpet pad start to break down quickly, and the longer materials stay saturated, the more of them need to be torn out instead of dried in place. That is why this page, and every page linked from it, tells you what to do while you wait for a crew.

If you are standing in water now, call (401) 598-8555 and then read the checklist below. Shut off the main water valve if the source is a pipe, turn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel without stepping in water, and keep people and pets out of anything that might be sewage.

The first hours

Your first 24 to 72 hours after water damage

These four steps follow the order a restoration job normally takes. Call dispatch at any point, including between steps, and a coordinator will tell you what to do next.

Stop the source

Shut off the main water valve, or the supply to the appliance, before anything else.

Make it safe

Keep out of rooms where water touches outlets or panels, and treat unknown water as contaminated.

Remove and dry

Extraction units pull standing water, then air movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure.

Repair

Once readings are normal, flooring, drywall and paint are rebuilt to match the rest of the home.

Take photos and short videos of the damage before anything is moved. Your insurer will ask for them, and your restoration records should match what you photographed.

Local knowledge

How Bristol's setting changes the water problem

Bristol is a peninsula town on Narragansett Bay with Bristol Harbor on one side and the Kickemuit River and Mount Hope Bay on the other.

The National Weather Service records that the 1938 hurricane pushed a storm surge of 12 to 15 feet into Narragansett Bay and that Hurricane Carol in 1954 sent surge of 10 to 15 feet across Rhode Island. For a homeowner today the takeaway is practical. Look up your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center before you assume a basement is safe.

Older housing matters too. Bristol includes the Waterfront Historic District, listed in 1975, and the Poppasquash Farms Historic District, listed in 1980, and many homes near Hope Street and the harbor were built long before modern drainage and plumbing. Plaster and lath ceilings, cut-stone foundations and cast-iron drain lines all react to water differently than newer drywall and PVC, so ask any restoration company how it handles older materials before it opens a wall.

Water and sewer service shapes the response as well.

The Bristol County Water Authority supplies Bristol, Warren and Barrington, and its water arrives through the East Bay Pipeline from the Providence Water Supply Board.

A main-line problem and a house-line problem are not the same call. A state environmental document on the town's wastewater plant describes wet-weather sewer overflows and names sump pumps tied into the sewer as a major cause. A basement that backs up in a heavy storm should be treated as a sewage problem until proven otherwise.

Know the water

What kind of water is on your floor

Restoration crews sort water into three categories because the category decides what can be dried and what must be thrown away.

CategoryTypical sourceWhat it usually means
Category 1Supply line, broken faucet, overflowing sink with clean waterDry in place if acted on quickly. Waiting turns clean water into Category 2.
Category 2Washing machine or dishwasher discharge, toilet overflow without solidsCarpet pad and porous materials are usually removed. Hard surfaces are cleaned and dried.
Category 3Sewage, river or harbor floodwater, long-standing waterTreated as contaminated. Porous materials come out and the area is cleaned and sanitized.

The three categories come from the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration. Ask any company you call whether its crews work to that standard.

Insurance

Water damage and your insurance policy

Documentation that supports your claim

Sudden and accidental water damage, such as a pipe that bursts, is often covered by a homeowners policy, while gradual leaks and neglect often are not. The Rhode Island Division of Insurance states that a homeowners policy does not cover flood, and that flood coverage is a separate policy that usually has a 30-day waiting period.

We document moisture readings, affected areas and the work performed so you have records to give your insurer. Check your policy or ask your insurer what applies to you. We do not promise that any claim will be approved.

When water damage leads to mold

Mold needs moisture, so a wall or ceiling that stays wet after a leak is where it starts. If you see dark spots or smell something musty after water damage, read the mold remediation page for how testing and removal follow the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard. Questions about health effects belong with a physician.

The most useful thing you can do about mold is dry the structure quickly, which is why the water comes first on this site and mold comes second.

Questions Bristol homeowners ask

How do I know if I need a restoration company or a plumber?

A plumber fixes the pipe, and a restoration company removes the water and dries the building. Many Bristol jobs need both, in that order. Shut off the water first, then call dispatch so drying can start while the plumber repairs the line.

Does restoration include basement waterproofing or foundation repair?

No. Restoration cleans up and dries water damage that already happened, and waterproofing stops future seepage. If your basement keeps taking water, you need a waterproofing contractor in addition to cleanup.

How long does drying take?

Drying commonly takes several days, but the number depends on the materials and the moisture readings, not a calendar. A crew checks readings with a moisture meter and removes equipment only when the structure is back to normal. Structural drying explains the equipment in plain words.

Do you rent air movers and dehumidifiers?

No. Equipment is part of a restoration job, set up and monitored by the crew, because equipment placed without moisture readings often leaves hidden wet spots behind.

What records help with a large loss?

Take dated photos and video before anything is moved, keep receipts for emergency steps, and write down which rooms and belongings were affected. Ask your insurer which documents it wants, and we provide moisture logs and a written scope for the work we do.

Can someone come the same day?

Same-day response is available when a crew is free, and dispatch is answered 24 hours a day. We do not promise a specific arrival time, since storms and road conditions around the bay change it.
