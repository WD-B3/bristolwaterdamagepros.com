Basement water damage cleanup is the removal of water and drying of a below-grade room after seepage, a sump failure, a leak from above or an outside flood. It covers floors, framing, finished walls and the belongings stored there.

This page is about the below-grade room. Rising floodwater is discussed at flood damage cleanup, and a crawl space under a house is a different structure, covered at crawl space water damage.

Water in the basement: stay safe, then act

Do not enter a basement with standing water if outlets, appliances or the electrical panel are in the water. Turn off power at the main only if you can reach the panel while standing on dry ground, and call the utility or 911 first if you are unsure. If the water is rising from outside, stay upstairs.

Once it is safe, move stored items to a dry area, photograph the water line and call dispatch for extraction. If the water came from a floor drain or toilet, treat it as sewage and see sewage backup cleanup.

What causes basement water in a bay town

Basement water comes from sump pump failure, floor drain backups, window wells, foundation seepage and plumbing leaks above. Heavy rain and storm surge raise groundwater near the bay, and a pump that quits during a power outage lets that water in.

A state environmental document on the Town of Bristol's wastewater system links wet-weather sewer overflows to stormwater entering the system, and names sump pumps connected to the sewer as a major cause. If your sump discharges into a floor drain or sewer line, ask your plumber or the town whether that is allowed. Check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your address.

Restoration and waterproofing are different jobs

Cleanup removes the water and dries the room. It does not keep water from coming back. If your basement takes water repeatedly, you may need a waterproofing contractor, drainage work or a better sump system, which are separate from restoration.

A restoration crew extracts water, removes wet carpet, drywall and insulation, and sets drying equipment. Concrete and block walls take time to dry, and a dehumidifier is run for several days.

  • Extraction of standing water.
  • Removal of wet drywall, insulation and flooring.
  • Drying of slab and block walls.
  • Contents moved, sorted and cleaned where possible.

The drying stage is described under structural drying, and the water heater is a common source covered by water heater leak cleanup.

Keeping the sump working

A sump pump is a mechanical device, and it can fail in a storm exactly when it is needed. Test it each season by pouring water into the pit. A battery backup keeps it running during a power outage, and a high-water alarm gives you a warning. Replace a pump that is more than ten years old.

Make sure the discharge line empties away from the foundation and not into the sewer, which can overload a municipal system in wet weather.

Common questions about basement water damage in Bristol

Will cleanup stop my basement from flooding again?

No. Cleanup dries what got wet, and waterproofing and drainage prevent future water. They are different trades.

Can I run a dehumidifier myself?

A household dehumidifier helps with humidity but will not dry a soaked slab and walls. Readings guide a crew.

What about my stored belongings?

Move them to a dry area, photograph them and keep a list for your insurer.

Do you handle basement water emergencies at night or on weekends in Bristol?

Yes. Dispatch is answered 24/7, including nights and storm weekends, and same-day response is available when a crew is free. Stay out of any water that touches outlets or the panel.
