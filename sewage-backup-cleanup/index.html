Emergency Sewage Backup Cleanup in Bristol, RI

A sewage backup is contaminated water, and cleanup has safety steps that clean-water jobs do not. We dispatch 24/7 for sewage backup cleanup in Bristol, RI.

Sewage backup cleanup is the safe removal and sanitizing of wastewater that has come back through a drain, toilet or floor drain. The water is classed as Category 3 under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, which means porous materials it touches are normally removed instead of dried.

This page is about contaminated water from a sewer or drain line. Clean-water leaks follow water extraction, and floodwater from outside is described under flood damage cleanup.

Gurgling drains and a smell from the floor drain

Sewage backups usually announce themselves before they flood a room. Slow drains in several fixtures, gurgling from a toilet or tub when another fixture runs, a sewer smell near a floor drain and water that returns after a flush are all early signs. Stop using water in the house when you notice them.

If a drain is already overflowing, keep everyone out of the room, especially children and pets. Do not try to wipe or vacuum raw sewage with household equipment.

Sewage backup emergency: stay out and call

Stop using sinks, toilets, showers and washers, since every flush adds to the problem. Turn off power to the room only if the panel is dry and reachable, and call the utility or 911 first if water touches outlets or the panel. Then call dispatch so containment and extraction can start.

Wear waterproof boots and gloves if you must enter, wash well afterward and discard food that was exposed. Questions about health effects belong with a physician.

What Bristol's sewer system means for backups

The Town of Bristol owns and runs its wastewater treatment plant at Plant Avenue, which discharges to Bristol Harbor. A state environmental document describes wet-weather sewer overflows in town, reports that flows rise sharply during storms and says sump pumps connected to the sewer are a major cause. If a heavy storm is what put sewage in your basement, tell the dispatcher, and also report it to the town.

Ask the town whether your property is on the municipal sewer, because the responsible party differs when the problem is in the main versus in your own lateral. A plumber can camera the lateral, while restoration handles the contaminated water inside the home.

How sewage cleanup is done safely

The crew wears protective gear, seals the work area, extracts the water and removes porous materials such as carpet, pad and wet drywall. Hard surfaces and framing are cleaned and sanitized, then dried with air movers and dehumidifiers until readings are normal.

  • Containment of the affected area.
  • Extraction of contaminated water.
  • Removal and disposal of porous materials.
  • Cleaning, sanitizing and drying of framing and hard surfaces.

When the backup reached a below-grade room, see basement water damage cleanup, and the drying stage follows structural drying.

What to do before the crew arrives

  • Keep people and pets out of the area.
  • Do not flush toilets or run water that drains to the backup.
  • Do not touch the water without protective gear.
  • Photograph the backup from a safe distance.

Sewage is Category 3 water under ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration.

Common questions about sewage backup cleanup in Bristol

Why can't I just bleach the floor?

Bleach does not make contaminated carpet or pad safe. Porous materials are removed, and the cleanup follows the S500 standard.

Who do I call about the sewer main?

Call the town for the municipal sewer and a plumber for the line inside your property. Restoration deals with what came into the house.

Does homeowners insurance cover sewage backup?

Often not unless the policy has a backup endorsement. Ask your insurer, and read your policy.

Do you handle sewage backup cleanup emergencies at night or on weekends in Bristol?

Yes. Dispatch is answered 24/7, including nights and weekends, and same-day response is available when a crew is free. Keep everyone out of the room and stop using water while you wait.
