How to Eliminate Mold in a Bathroom in Bristol, RI

Bathroom mold is usually a ventilation problem first and a cleaning problem second.

By the Bristol Water Damage Pros team

Emergency Dispatch
(401) 598-8555
Call Now

A bathroom produces moisture every day, and when the room cannot dry between showers, mold grows on grout, caulk, ceilings and the backs of fixtures. Most bathroom mold is on surface layers that can be cleaned, but some is a sign of a hidden leak that needs a plumber and a restoration crew.

The advice below separates the surface problem from the hidden one.

Clean the surface the right way

Use detergent and water on grout, tile and a shower curtain, scrub gently and rinse. Replace caulk that is dark or peeling, since mold grows inside it. Wear gloves and a respirator and ventilate the room while you work.

Fix the ventilation

A fan that does not move air or that vents into the attic leaves moisture behind. Run the fan during the shower and for a while after. Leave the door open so the room can dry. If your bathroom has no fan, ask a contractor about adding one that vents outside.

Look for a hidden leak

If mold returns quickly, check for leaks at the shower valve, around the tub, under the sink and from the room above. A stained ceiling below a bathroom, a soft floor near the tub or a musty smell with no visible source suggests water behind the surface.

  • Soft or spongy floor near the tub or toilet.
  • Stain on the ceiling of the room below.
  • Paint that bubbles or peels near the shower.
  • Grout that crumbles and stays wet.

When to call a professional

Call a remediation company when mold is on drywall or ceilings, covers a large area or follows a leak. A crew can open the wall, find the moisture and dry it. See mold remediation and wall repair.

Keep it from coming back

Squeegee the shower walls, hang towels to dry and fix drips promptly. Use a humidity meter to check that the room stays under a moderate level. A small dehumidifier in a basement bath can help.

Shower habits that help

Run the fan before you turn on the water and keep it on for at least twenty minutes after you finish. Open the door afterward so humid air can leave the room. If you can, squeegee the walls and glass, and hang towels outside the bathroom to dry.

Wash shower curtains and liners regularly, or replace them when they stay stained. Soap residue feeds growth, so rinsing surfaces matters as much as drying them.

Caulk, grout and sealant

Caulk wears out in a few years, and failed caulk lets water behind tile and into the wall. Cut out old caulk, clean and dry the joint fully and apply a new bead. Check grout for cracks, and reseal it where the manufacturer advises.

Tile that sounds hollow or feels loose often means water has gotten behind it. That is a sign to stop cosmetic fixes and have the wall checked.

Ventilation upgrades

A fan should be rated for the size of the room and vent to the outside, not into an attic. A ducted fan with a timer or humidity sensor takes the guesswork out of it. If your fan is loud or weak, replacing it is often inexpensive compared with repeated mold cleanup.

Hidden moisture behind tile

A wall can be wet behind tile for months while the surface looks fine. Signs include loose tile, a spongy feel, discolored grout and a musty smell. A moisture meter can read through some surfaces, and a thermal camera can show cool, damp areas.

If the wall behind the shower is wet, the shower valve, the drain or the waterproofing is the likely cause. A plumber and a tile contractor may be needed, along with a restoration crew to dry the wall.

A simple weekly routine

Spend two minutes after each shower to squeegee the walls and wipe the sink. Once a week, wash the curtain or liner and wipe down the grout lines. Once a month, check under the sink and around the toilet base for damp spots. Small habits keep a bathroom from becoming a project.

Small projects that pay off

Replace a cracked grout line, swap a failing exhaust fan and repaint the ceiling with a moisture-resistant paint. These tasks cost far less than rebuilding a wall. They also give you the chance to look for soft spots and hidden stains while the work is open.

If you rent, report a failing fan or a leak to your landlord in writing and keep a copy.

When to involve your landlord or association

Renters and condominium owners often share walls, ceilings and plumbing with neighbors. If the source of the moisture is above or beside you, report it in writing and keep a copy. Include dates and photos.

Ask who is responsible for repairs and for drying, since the lease or the association rules decide. Do not open walls that belong to someone else without permission.

Professional mold removal follows ANSI/IICRC S520, the published standard for mold remediation, when the growth is more than a small surface patch.

Bristol bathrooms in older homes

Many homes in Bristol's Waterfront Historic District, listed in 1975, were built long before modern bath fans. Older bathrooms often rely on a window for ventilation, which does little in winter.

The Bristol County Water Authority supplies Bristol, Warren and Barrington, and all of its customers are metered. A rising water bill with no change in use can point to a hidden leak, which is worth checking when mold keeps returning.

More questions about this topic

Is a window enough ventilation in an older Bristol bathroom?

Often not in cold months, when people keep it shut. A fan that vents outside handles moisture year round.
