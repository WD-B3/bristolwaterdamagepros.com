How to Shut Off Your Home Water Main in Bristol, RI

Finding the main valve takes a minute on a calm day and saves thousands in a burst.

By the Bristol Water Damage Pros team

Emergency Dispatch
(401) 598-8555
Call Now

When a pipe fails, the first job is to stop the water, and the first tool is the main shutoff valve. Many homeowners have never located theirs. Spending a few minutes to find it, test it and label it is one of the cheapest forms of protection a home has.

This guide explains where valves usually are and what to do when yours will not close.

Where the main valve usually is

In many homes the main sits where the water line enters the house, often in a basement or utility room near the front wall, near the water heater or beside the meter. In homes without a basement it may be in a closet, garage or crawl space. Look for a round wheel handle or a lever.

Types of valves

A gate valve has a round wheel you turn clockwise to close. A ball valve has a lever, and a quarter turn closes it. Gate valves in older homes can stick or break when forced. Turn gently, and if the valve does not move, stop.

Test it before you need it

Close the valve fully, open a faucet to confirm the water stops, then reopen the valve slowly. If the valve leaks or sticks, call a plumber to replace it. Tag the valve with a bright label so anyone in the house can find it.

If the valve will not turn

Do not force it. Shut off the individual fixture or appliance valves that you can reach, such as under the sink or behind the toilet. Call a plumber and tell dispatch if water is spreading. The street-side valve is controlled by the water utility, and the utility can shut it off.

  • Shut individual angle stops under sinks and behind toilets.
  • Turn off the water heater if water is near it.
  • Move belongings out of the water's path.
  • Call the water utility or a plumber.

After the water is off

Open a faucet at the lowest level to drain the lines, photograph the damage and call a restoration company. See burst pipe damage and water extraction for next steps.

Shutting off the water heater and appliances

If water is running near the water heater, turn off its power or gas supply according to the manufacturer's instructions before draining it. Appliances such as dishwashers, washers and ice makers have their own shutoff valves, and closing them can stop a smaller leak without turning off the whole house.

Ask your plumber to add shutoff valves anywhere they are missing. A fixture with its own valve is far easier to isolate in an emergency.

Preparing for winter

In a region with hard freezes, know how to shut off outdoor spigots and drain the lines. If you will be away, consider shutting off the main and draining the system. A frozen pipe in an empty house can run for days before anyone notices.

Keep the heat on at a safe minimum, open cabinet doors on exterior walls and insulate pipes in cold spaces such as crawl spaces, garages and cellars.

Smart shutoff devices

Some homes add leak sensors and automatic shutoff valves that close the main when water is detected. They are not a replacement for knowing your manual valve, but they can reduce damage when no one is home. Ask a licensed plumber what fits your system.

What to do right after you close the valve

Open the lowest faucet in the house to drain the lines, and flush a toilet to clear the tank. Check the water heater. If it is electric, shut off its breaker so the elements do not burn out. Stay clear of wet electrical equipment.

Then walk the house and note every wet spot. Take photos, move belongings and call a restoration company if water has spread.

Labeling and teaching the household

Put a bright tag on the main valve and tell everyone in the house where it is, including older children and visitors who stay a while. Take a photo and keep it on your phone. If you rent, tell the landlord if the valve is hard to reach or stiff.

Common mistakes

Homeowners often turn the wrong valve, close a valve only partway or forget to reopen it slowly. Another mistake is waiting to call for help while trying to fix a burst line. Shut the water, make the area safe and call, then let the professionals handle the rest.

What to tell your family

Everyone old enough to help should know where the valve is and how to turn it. Show them in person, not just on a diagram. Practice once, and reopen it slowly.

Post the dispatch number near the valve so it is easy to find when you are rushing. A calm plan makes a stressful moment easier.

Water service in Bristol

The Bristol County Water Authority supplies Bristol, Warren and Barrington with water from Providence Water through the East Bay Pipeline, in service since 1998. It has over 17,000 customers, and every customer is metered.

Because customers are metered, your meter can help you find a hidden leak. Shut off all water use, then check whether the meter's dial is still moving. If it is, water is going somewhere.

Yes. All Bristol County Water Authority customers are metered, so if the meter dial moves with every tap off, water is escaping somewhere.
