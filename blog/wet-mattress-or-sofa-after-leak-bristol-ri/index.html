Wet Mattress or Sofa After a Leak in Bristol, RI

Clean water caught fast gives furniture a chance. Contaminated water or time takes it away.

By the Bristol Water Damage Pros team

Emergency Dispatch
(401) 598-8555
Call Now

A wet mattress or sofa is one of the first things people try to save after a leak. Foam, batting and fabric hold water deep inside, and surface drying does not reach it. Whether it can be saved depends on what kind of water it was, how long it sat and what the piece is made of.

The guidance below helps you decide quickly.

First, what kind of water?

Clean water from a supply line gives you a chance. Water from a washing machine, a toilet or a flood is contaminated and soft furnishings that absorb it are usually discarded. Water categories follow the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, explained in plain terms at water extraction.

If it was clean water

Get it off the wet floor and raised so air reaches it. Blot, do not rub. Press towels into the cushions to pull out water. Use fans and a dehumidifier in the room. Wet foam may take several days to dry, and if it stays damp past a day or two, mold becomes more likely.

Mattresses

Stand a mattress on its edge, blot out as much water as possible and dry it with air movement and low heat. Do not use high heat, which can damage foam. If the mattress stayed wet for more than a day or smells musty, replacement is often better.

Sofas and upholstered furniture

Remove cushions, prop the frame up and dry the underside. Wood frames can warp and stain fabric. Leather and microfiber react differently. A professional cleaner can sometimes extract water and treat upholstery, but the cost may exceed the price of a replacement.

Document before you discard

Photograph the item, note the brand and age and keep a tag or label. Your insurer may want to see it or want the photos. Do not throw out a large item until you have documented it.

  • Photograph and label each damaged piece.
  • Keep tags and receipts if you have them.
  • Ask your insurer whether to keep items for inspection.
  • Dry the room so it does not damage other furniture.

The room itself needs drying too, which is covered at structural drying and carpet water damage.

Drying methods that work and ones that do not

Air movement and dehumidification work best. A hair dryer on high heat can damage foam and spread moisture unevenly. A wet vacuum can pull water out of upholstery, but it will not reach deep layers. Sunlight outdoors helps when the weather is dry and the item is clean-water wet.

Baking soda sprinkled over a damp mattress can absorb some moisture and odor, then it should be vacuumed once dry. It is a helper, not a cure.

Odor and mildew

A musty smell after drying suggests moisture remains inside. Another round of drying may help, but if the smell returns, replacement is usually the practical choice. Sleeping on a mattress that holds mildew is not worth the risk to your comfort.

Replacement and insurance

If you replace an item, keep the receipt. Ask your insurer how personal property is valued, replacement cost or actual cash value, since the difference can be large. Photos of the damaged item and its label support the claim.

Pillows, rugs and soft items

Small items are easier. Pillows and stuffed items that got clean water can often go through the wash and dry cycle. Area rugs can be extracted and dried outdoors or in a well-ventilated room. Items that smell of sewage or were soaked in floodwater should be discarded.

Wool and natural fiber rugs can shrink or bleed color when wet. A professional cleaner can advise on high-value pieces.

Protecting the floor under the furniture

Wet furniture left on a floor can stain it and trap moisture underneath. Lift legs onto foil or blocks, and check the floor under the piece for dampness. Wood floors and carpet pad hold water that you cannot see, so a moisture reading is worth the effort.

A quick decision guide

  • Clean water and caught within a day, dry and inspect.
  • Gray or black water, discard.
  • Musty after drying, replace.
  • High-value or antique piece, ask a specialist.
  • Unsure, photograph and ask dispatch.

Whatever you decide, document before you discard.

When to ask for help

If the furniture is valuable, if you are unsure of the water category or if you cannot dry the room yourself, call. A restoration crew can extract water from upholstery and carpet and set dehumidifiers that dry the whole room.

Tell dispatch what is wet, how long it has been wet and whether anyone has walked through it. That helps the crew decide what to bring.

Preventing a repeat

Check hoses, supply lines and drain pans around your appliances each year. Replace cracked or bulging hoses. Place furniture on risers if the room has a history of water. A few simple steps protect your belongings from the next leak.

If a room floods more than once, consider storing sensitive items higher up.

Humidity and drying on the bay

Rhode Island's coastal air is humid in summer, and the National Weather Service recorded 6 to 10 inches of rain in March 2010. In damp weather, fabric and foam dry slowly, so a dehumidifier is more important than fans alone.

The Rhode Island Division of Insurance advises insured people to photograph damage, which applies to furniture too. The Division's phone is (401) 462-9520.
