Flood damage restoration is the cleanup, drying and repair of a structure after rising water from storms, tides, rivers or runoff enters it. Unlike a pipe leak, floodwater carries soil, bacteria and debris, so the work includes removal of porous materials and sanitizing of what remains.

This page is for water that came from outside or rose from the ground. A plain roof or window leak is covered under storm water damage, and a below-grade room has its own page at basement water damage cleanup.

Flood emergency: staying safe before cleanup

Do not walk through floodwater to reach a panel or shut-off, and never use electrical equipment while standing in water. Turn off power at the main only if you can reach the panel dry. If you smell gas or see sparks, leave and call 911 and the utility before calling us.

Floodwater is Category 3. Wear boots and gloves if you must enter, keep children and pets out, and do not eat food that touched it. Call dispatch so extraction and containment can begin as soon as the site is safe.

Bristol's flood history and what it means for you

Bristol sits on Narragansett Bay, and the National Weather Service records that the 1938 hurricane drove a 12 to 15 foot storm surge into the bay. Hurricane Carol in 1954 brought 10 to 15 feet of surge to Rhode Island. In March 2010, six to 10 inches of rain over three days led to a federal disaster declaration for every Rhode Island county.

Those events are the reason to look up your own address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center. Poppasquash, the harbor streets and the Mount Hope shoreline sit on the bay, and other parts of town sit higher. A flood zone label tells you about insurance and rebuild rules, and the town building inspection office at 10 Court Street can explain what applies to repairs.

What a flood cleanup crew does

The crew first extracts the water, then removes materials that cannot be cleaned: carpet and pad, wet insulation, and drywall that wicked contaminated water. Hard surfaces and framing are cleaned and sanitized, then dried with air movers and dehumidifiers.

  • Water extraction and removal of debris.
  • Disposal of porous materials soaked by Category 3 water.
  • Cleaning and sanitizing of framing and hard surfaces.
  • Drying with air movers and LGR dehumidifiers until readings are normal.

Standing water comes out first under water extraction, and the drying stage follows structural drying. If sewage mixed with the flood, see sewage backup cleanup for the extra safety steps.

Common questions about flood damage cleanup in Bristol

Is floodwater the same as water from a burst pipe?

No. Pipe water starts clean, while floodwater carries contaminants from outside, so materials it soaks are usually removed instead of dried.

Does homeowners insurance cover flood damage?

The Rhode Island Division of Insurance states that homeowners insurance does not cover flood. Flood coverage is separate and usually has a 30-day waiting period, so ask your agent.

How can I check my flood zone?

Use the FEMA Flood Map Service Center and enter your address. The town building inspection office can explain what the zone means for repairs.

Do you handle flood damage cleanup emergencies at night or on weekends in Bristol?

Yes. Dispatch is answered 24/7, including during storms when the roads allow, and same-day response is available when a crew is free. We do not promise an arrival time because flooded roads can delay any crew.
