Slab leak water damage is the wetting of flooring, subfloor and walls by a water line that runs under or inside a concrete slab. Slabs show up in additions, garages, sunrooms and some newer homes, and a leak under one can stay hidden while it undermines flooring above.

Whether your home has a slab at all depends on how it was built, and many Bristol homes sit on basements or crawl spaces instead. If your leak is at a basement or under-floor space, see basement water damage cleanup or crawl space water damage.

A warm spot on the floor or a meter that keeps running

People rarely search for the words slab leak first. They notice a warm patch on the floor, a hissing sound in a quiet house, flooring that buckles for no reason, a damp baseboard or a water bill that jumps. Another test is to turn off every fixture and watch the water meter. If it keeps moving, water is leaking somewhere.

These signs point to a line under the floor, but they can also point to a leak inside a wall. A plumber's leak detection equipment finds the exact spot, and restoration begins after the supply is shut off.

Slab leak emergency: shut off the main first

If water is rising through a floor or you hear a running sound, shut off the main water valve immediately. Move belongings off the affected flooring and stay out of rooms where water meets electrical outlets or baseboard heaters. Call the utility or 911 first for any electrical risk, then call dispatch.

A broken line under a slab pushes water through the floor and joints, and it can wet walls from below. The longer it runs, the more water reaches adjoining rooms, so a quick shut-off matters more than anything else.

How restoration and plumbing divide the work

A plumber finds and repairs the leak. That repair can involve opening the slab, rerouting the line outside the slab or replacing a section, and it is a separate scope from restoration. The restoration crew extracts water, dries the slab surface, subfloor and walls and removes flooring that cannot be dried.

Concrete holds moisture for a long time, so drying a slab uses dehumidifiers and moisture readings over several days. Wood flooring installed over a slab is the first thing to cup or buckle, which is why hardwood floor water damage and carpet water damage are common companions to a slab leak.

  • Shut-off confirmation and leak detection by a plumber.
  • Extraction of water on the slab and removal of wet flooring.
  • Dehumidification of the slab and adjoining walls.
  • Moisture readings until the slab is within normal range.

Common questions about slab leak water damage in Bristol

Can I fix a slab leak without opening the floor?

That is a plumber's decision. Some leaks are repaired by rerouting a line, and others require access through the slab.

Is a high water bill enough to suspect a slab leak?

It is a warning sign, not proof. Check the meter with all fixtures off, then have a plumber confirm.

How long does a slab take to dry?

It varies with thickness and moisture readings, and it often takes longer than a wall. A crew monitors readings until the slab is back to normal.

Yes. Dispatch is answered 24/7, including nights and weekends, and same-day response is available when a crew is free. Shut off the main valve and tell the coordinator what you see.
