Fire district water, wells and permits

The Stone Bridge Fire District, supplied from Stafford Pond, serves about half of northern Tiverton, directly or through the Tiverton and North Tiverton Fire Districts. Properties outside it may use private wells. Permits go through the Tiverton Building Department at 343 Highland Road, (401) 625-6715.

If your home has a well, a flood can contaminate it, so ask dispatch and your local health office about testing before drinking from it. Check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your address.

Insurance questions for Tiverton owners

Flood is not covered by a standard homeowners policy and flood coverage usually has a 30-day waiting period, per the Rhode Island Division of Insurance. Photograph damage first and ask your insurer. No claim outcome is promised.

How a Tiverton job usually runs

Call dispatch with your address and a short description. The coordinator explains what to shut off and how a crew would reach you. The crew checks safety, takes readings and explains scope and price before work begins.

Typical steps are extraction, removal of wet materials that cannot be saved and drying with air movers and dehumidifiers. Moisture readings guide the crew until the structure is dry, then repairs follow.

Homes with wells and septic systems

A property outside district water service may use a private well and a septic system. Flooding can affect both. If a flood reaches your well head or septic area, avoid drinking the water until it has been tested, and ask a professional about inspecting the system.

Water that backs up through a drain on a septic system is contaminated and should be handled by trained crews.

Rural and waterfront roads

Storms can close a road anywhere near the water or knock down a tree, which affects crew travel. Tell dispatch about any closure or hazard on your road so the crew can plan the safest route.

Keep a flashlight, a charged phone and a list of important numbers handy during storm season.

Planning for the next storm

After a loss, take steps to reduce the next one. Raise stored items, service the sump and well pump, trim trees near the house and check the roof and gutters. Keep a list of contacts where everyone in the house can find it.

If you are on a private well, keep bottled water on hand during storm season in case the well is affected.

Tell dispatch if your property is on a private or unpaved road, so the crew can plan the right vehicle and route.
