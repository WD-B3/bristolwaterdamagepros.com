Water damage in downtown Bristol: what to expect

Older buildings

The Bristol Waterfront Historic District covers the original core of town, laid out in 1680. It holds homes, civic buildings and shops from three centuries.

Harbor and storm exposure

East Bay RI reported in October 2021 that sea-level projections put much of Thames Street at risk from major storms.

Storm drains and sewer pressure

During the remnants of Hurricane Ida in September 2021, East Bay RI reported sewer pressure breaking up pavement on Hope Street near Wood Street.

Shared walls and floors

Shops, restaurants and apartments often sit in the same building, so one leak can reach more than one tenant.

Why water behaves differently downtown

The Bristol Waterfront Historic District runs from Bristol Harbor to the east side of Wood Street, with Washington Street on the north and Walker Cove on the south. It was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in March 1975. Homes, civic buildings and commercial buildings sit close together within those blocks, and many were built long before modern waterproofing.

Older construction changes how a crew dries a building. Plaster and lath walls, wood floors over deep joists and brick or stone cellars hold moisture for a long time. A surface that feels dry can still be wet inside, so crews take readings in framing and subfloors and not only on the finish.

Storms, tides and the harbor

Downtown sits at the water's edge. The National Weather Service records that the 1938 hurricane pushed a storm surge of 12 to 15 feet into Narragansett Bay. East Bay RI reported in October 2021 that projections for a 100-year storm showed areas such as Independence Park and large parts of Thames Street underwater by anywhere from four to 16 feet.

Those figures describe scenarios and not a forecast for any one street. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. To see the flood zone for a single address, search it on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center.

Before We Arrive downtown

Stop the source

Close the nearest shutoff valve. In a shared building, tell the manager which valve serves your unit.

Mind the power

Keep out of any room where water touches outlets. Never reach for a breaker while standing in water.

Protect stock and papers

Lift small items, files and inventory off wet floors. Leave heavy furniture where it is.

Photograph first

Record the water line, the ceiling and the contents before anything is moved or thrown out.

Shared walls, shops and apartments

Many downtown buildings have a shop or restaurant on the ground floor and apartments above. Water from an upstairs fixture can run through ceilings and walls into the units below. If you manage or rent in a mixed-use building, tell dispatch which floors are wet and whether neighboring units have been checked.

For a business, the commercial water damage restoration page explains how work can be planned around downtime. A burst pipe inside a shared wall is one way a small leak becomes a large one.

Permits, historic buildings and who to call

Before you repair or replace materials, ask the Bristol Building Inspection Department which permits apply. It is at 10 Court Street, (401) 253-7000, extension 127. If your building is inside the historic district, ask the town whether any review applies before you change original or exterior features.

Water service in Bristol comes from the Bristol County Water Authority, which also serves Warren and Barrington. If the problem starts at the street shutoff or a water main, the authority is the party to contact, and dispatch can help you find the shutoff inside your own building.

Insurance questions after a downtown loss

The Rhode Island Division of Insurance says a standard homeowners policy does not cover flood. A separate flood policy usually carries a 30-day waiting period, and National Flood Insurance Program limits are $250,000 for a building and $100,000 for contents. Photograph the damage and ask your insurer what applies. No claim outcome is promised.

Services most used in this part of town

Common requests downtown are water extraction, flood damage cleanup, storm water damage cleanup and commercial restoration. The main Bristol water damage restoration page and the service area overview show how the rest of the coverage fits together.

What shapes the cost of this work

Costs downtown follow the same factors as anywhere else in Bristol.

Building age

Plaster, old framing and masonry take longer to dry than newer materials.

Shared structure

A leak that crosses units means more rooms and more equipment.

Water source

Clean supply water costs less to handle than harbor or sewer water.

Repair stage

Rebuilding is separate from drying and is quoted on its own.

Common questions from Downtown Bristol

Is downtown Bristol in a FEMA flood zone?

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. Zones differ from one property to the next, so check your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center or ask your insurer.

Does water damage in a historic building need special handling?

Older plaster, wood and masonry dry slowly, so crews measure moisture in the framing before equipment comes out. Ask the Building Inspection Department about permits or review before you replace original materials.

Who do I call first for a burst pipe in a downtown apartment?

Shut off the water if you can reach the valve, then call dispatch and say which floors are wet. Tell your landlord or building manager as well so other units can be checked.

Do you work in restaurants and shops downtown?

Yes. Commercial jobs are planned around downtime so a business can return to normal as soon as the structure is dry. The commercial water damage page explains the steps.
