How dispatch covers the East Bay

Dispatch is answered in Bristol at all hours. When you call, the coordinator asks for your address, the source of the water and what it has touched. Crews travel from Bristol along Route 114 and nearby roads to the towns on this page.

Travel time depends on traffic, weather and bridge conditions. We do not promise a set arrival time, but same-day response is available when a crew is free.

What each town page covers

Each town page lists the town's single ZIP code, the local building department, the water supplier and the typical water problems in that town. It also explains how storm and flood risk differs along the bay, the river and inland roads.

  • Warren: river and tidal exposure, older mill-town buildings.
  • Portsmouth: shoreline storms and seasonal homes on Aquidneck Island.
  • Barrington: basements and sump pumps in low lots.
  • Tiverton: waterfront homes, wells and rural roads.

Beyond these towns

If your property is outside the towns listed, call and ask. Some locations are reachable by a short drive, and others involve a bridge or ferry. A coordinator will tell you honestly whether a crew can get to you and how long it is likely to take.

Before you call

Have your address, the source of the water, the rooms affected and the time you first noticed it. If you know where your main shutoff is, say so. If you cannot find it, tell the coordinator and they will guide you.

While you wait for a crew, keep out of rooms where water touches outlets, move small items off the floor and photograph the damage.

Why a local base matters

A crew that works from the same peninsula as the call knows the roads, the building stock and the weather. Bristol sits on Narragansett Bay, and a storm that floods one neighborhood may leave another untouched. That local knowledge helps a crew plan the route and the work.

Route 114 and the Mount Hope Bridge

Bristol sits on a peninsula, and most traffic into and out of town follows Route 114. The Mount Hope Bridge, opened in 1929, links Bristol to Aquidneck Island, so crews heading to Portsmouth use it. A closure or a heavy storm can slow that route, which is why dispatch asks you to describe your location carefully.

Crews heading north to Warren and Barrington use the same corridor. Tiverton lies across the water to the southeast, and the route depends on current road and bridge conditions.

Planning ahead for storm season

The National Weather Service records that the 1938 hurricane pushed a storm surge of 12 to 15 feet into Narragansett Bay and that Hurricane Carol in 1954 sent a surge of 10 to 15 feet across Rhode Island. Those events are a reminder that bay towns can see serious flooding.

Save the dispatch number in your phone before the season starts, and share it with everyone in the household.

How to choose between towns on this page

If you live in Warren, start with the Warren page. If you are in Portsmouth, use the Portsmouth page, and so on. If you live between two towns, either page will help, and dispatch can confirm the details when you call.

Each page uses the town's own building department and water supplier, so you can check permits and utility contacts for your address.

Service-area questions we hear

People ask whether a crew can reach them on a holiday, during a storm or at night. Dispatch is answered 24 hours a day, every day of the year. Whether a crew is free depends on the number of calls, and a coordinator will be honest about it.

Others ask whether we serve their neighboring town. If it is not on this page, call and ask. We would rather tell you plainly than send a crew that cannot get to you.

If your town is not listed, call and tell us where you are. We will say honestly whether we can reach you and how.

For homeowners who live near the water, the flood and storm guidance on each town page is a good place to start before the next season.
