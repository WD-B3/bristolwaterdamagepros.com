Warren permits, water and tidal streets

Water service in Warren comes from the Bristol County Water Authority, which also serves Bristol and Barrington. Building permits for repairs in Warren go through the Warren Building and Zoning office at 514 Main Street, (401) 245-7343. Ask the office whether your rebuild needs a permit before you order materials.

The state's sea-level planning fact sheet for Warren flags Main, Market, Child and Water Streets and Kickemuit Road as areas exposed to tidal flooding, along with the Belchers Cove Bridge on Market Street and the Kickamuit bridge on Child Street. If you live near those streets, check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your address.

What to ask your insurer after a Warren loss

A standard homeowners policy does not cover flood, and flood coverage is a separate policy that usually has a 30-day waiting period, according to the Rhode Island Division of Insurance. Photograph the damage first and ask your insurer what applies. No claim outcome is promised.

How a Warren job usually runs

A call to dispatch starts with the street address and what the water touched. A coordinator tells you what to shut off, then a crew travels north on Route 114 from the Bristol base. On arrival the crew checks safety, takes readings and explains the scope and price before work begins.

Most Warren jobs begin with extraction. Pumps and extraction units remove standing water, and wet materials that cannot be saved are removed. Drying equipment is then set and monitored until readings return to normal.

Older Warren buildings and what water does to them

Older houses and mixed-use buildings often have plaster walls, wood floors laid over wide joists and basements with stone or brick foundations. These materials absorb water slowly and release it slowly. A floor that looks dry can still hold moisture in the joists below it.

Because of that, crews take readings in framing and subfloors, not only on the surface. Plaster that is soaked may have to be removed, while plaster that is merely damp can sometimes be dried and patched.

Checking your address before the next storm

Look up your property on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center and compare it with the streets the state planning fact sheet names. If you are on or near one of them, raise stored items, clear drains and ask your insurer about flood coverage, which usually has a 30-day waiting period.
