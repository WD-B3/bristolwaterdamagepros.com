Where Bristol Highlands sits

Bristol Highlands is a neighborhood name used by several real-estate guides, and it sits on the west side of Bristol near Narragansett Bay. The guides describe a mix of large and modest lots. The name is a local label and not a legal boundary, so check that your street fits.

With a median year built of 1976, many homes here are old enough to need a new water heater, new supply lines or new roofing. Some of the oldest parts may be original while newer additions use modern materials, and the two meet at joints where leaks like to start.

Heavy rain and drainage

The National Weather Service recorded 6 to 10 inches of rain in the March 2010 storm, which shows how much water a spring storm can bring. When storm drains and sump pumps are overwhelmed, water can enter low windows, basement walls and garage doors. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified.

Keep gutters, downspouts and window wells clear, and test your sump pump before wet seasons. A battery backup matters most when the power goes out during the storm.

Finishes and contents worth documenting

Hardwood, built-in cabinetry and finished trim can cost a lot to replace. Photograph each room now, before a loss, and keep the images off the device that might be damaged. After a loss, photograph again before anything is moved.

The hardwood floor water damage repair page explains cupping and drying, and the kitchen water damage restoration page covers cabinets and flooring under appliances. A written list of damaged items helps your insurer review the claim.

Before We Arrive in Bristol Highlands

Stop the water

Close the valve nearest the source. If you cannot find it, say so on the call.

Mind electrical hazards

Keep out of rooms where water touches outlets. Do not touch the panel while standing in water.

Protect the finishes

Lift rugs, art and small furniture off wet floors if the path is dry.

Start a list

Write down damaged items with a rough age and value while you remember them.

Older systems in a mixed-age neighborhood

A house with a 1970s core and newer additions has plumbing of several ages. Check under sinks and behind appliances once a season for drips, stains or soft cabinet floors. A small leak can run for weeks before it shows, and slow leaks often cause more hidden damage than sudden ones.

If a pipe does fail, a burst pipe cleanup starts with shutting off the water and removing what is standing. Ask a plumber to repair the line, since restoration does not replace plumbing work.

Permits and insurance

Ask the Bristol Building Inspection Department which permits apply before you rebuild. It is at 10 Court Street, (401) 253-7000, extension 127. The Rhode Island Division of Insurance says flood is not covered by homeowners policies and that flood policies carry a 30-day waiting period.

Keep receipts for emergency steps and ask your insurer which records it wants. We provide moisture logs and a written scope for the work we do, and we do not promise that any claim will be approved.

Common questions from Bristol Highlands

Is Bristol Highlands in a FEMA flood zone?

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. Search your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center or ask your insurer.

What should I document before and after a loss?

Photograph each room and list major contents now. After a loss, photograph the water line and damaged items before anything is moved, and keep receipts for emergency steps.

Can hardwood floors be saved after a leak?

Sometimes. Boards that are dried quickly and measured may flatten again, while boards that stay wet can cup, crown or buckle. A moisture reading shows whether drying is working.

Do you work on kitchens with built-in cabinets?

Yes. Water under cabinets and appliances can hide behind toe kicks, so crews check those areas and dry them. Cabinets that are swollen or delaminated may need to be replaced.
